Note: $t0, $t1, $t2, $t3 are reused as scratch registers across
different sections of the program (this is intentional -- MIPS
$t-registers are "temporary"/caller-saved by convention, meaning
they're not expected to survive a function call or a later
section). Their FINAL values reflect whichever instruction wrote
them LAST in program order, not their values mid-loop. If you're
debugging register-by-register, check them at the point in time
that matches your trace, not just at full completion:
  $t0 = 5     (last write: index reaching a1=5 inside find_max)
  $t1 = 80    (last write: current_addr inside find_max, 64+16)
  $t2 = 1     (last write: slti result near the end of main)
  $t3 = 128   (last write: results base address near the end)

=== Execution notes ===
- The program halts by spinning forever at the "done: j done"
  label -- this is the standard MIPS convention for "end of
  program" since there's no OS to return to. When testing on your
  CPU, run until you see PC stop changing (settled on the same
  address, repeatedly re-fetching the same "j done" instruction),
  then check registers/memory.
